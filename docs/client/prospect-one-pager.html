Accessibility, in plain terms

Accessibility means everyone can use your website or app, including people who are blind, deaf, have low vision or can't use a mouse. About 1 in 4 U.S. adults has a disability (CDC).

Why

Complaints and lawsuits

Thousands of U.S. accessibility lawsuits are filed every year. An audit shows where you stand.

New legal deadlines

ADA Title II (Apr 2027–28): state and local governments, public universities, special districts. EU Accessibility Act (2025): businesses selling to EU consumers. Section 508: federal agencies and vendors.

Contracts and RFPs

Buyers ask for a VPAT or proof of WCAG AA before they sign.

A better product

Clear, accessible design means fewer support tickets and more completed checkouts.

A, AA and AAA: what the levels mean

Criteria come from WCAG 2.2, the W3C’s web standard: 86 pass/fail criteria in three levels, each including the levels below it.

  1. Level A

    Can people use it at all?

    The bare minimum (31 criteria).

    • Works with a keyboard alone
    • Images have text descriptions
    • Videos have captions

    Effort: lowest (code fixes).
    Return: removes blockers; rarely enough alone for legal or contract needs.

  2. Level AA Recommended

    Can people use it comfortably?

    What laws and contracts cite (24 more).

    • Text has enough contrast to read
    • Works when zoomed to 200%
    • Keyboard focus is always visible

    Added effort: moderate (design and front-end, done with A).
    Return: meets laws and contracts; the biggest drop in legal risk.

  3. Level AAA

    Is it the best it can be?

    The highest level (31 more criteria).

    • Stronger contrast (7:1)
    • Sign language for video
    • Plain language, no time limits

    Added effort: high, ongoing (content, media, brand colors).
    Return: diminishing site-wide (the W3C doesn't advise it); worth it for key flows in healthcare, government, education.

Common questions

Can't we just use a plugin?

Plugins and scanners catch only 30–40% of issues. We add hands-on keyboard and screen reader testing.

Do you fix issues, or just find them?

Both. You get developer-ready tickets and a phased plan; we fix or support your team, then re-test.

What do you need from us?

Your key pages or sitemap, test accounts for each user role, staging access, and code access.
